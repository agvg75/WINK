WINK

Worm Imaging & Kinematics

A free toolset for measuring C. elegans behaviour and physiology from video and confocal imaging — built so you can see inside every measurement it makes.

Vidal-Gadea Lab · Illinois State University

What it is

WINK grew out of a single worm tracker and became a full lab bench: roughly forty tools spanning locomotion, sensory behaviour, rhythmic programs, calcium imaging, and muscle morphometry. They all launch from one hub and share one interface, so a student who learns one tool already knows how the rest behave.

It is for labs that want tracking and analysis they can inspect and argue with, rather than a black box that returns a number. Some tools are marked Experimental — usable, but not yet validated to the same standard. WINK says so rather than hiding it.

How it thinks

You approve it, or it isn't data

Automatic detection is a first pass you accept, edit, or override — never a silent final answer.

Calibration is required, not assumed

Missing scale or frame rate stops a measurement. A wrong scale makes every result wrong by the same factor, plausibly and invisibly.

Corrections are kept

Every override is logged, building a real record of where the automatic detectors can and cannot be trusted.

A failure is not a zero

When detection finds nothing, WINK says so — instead of writing a zero that reads later like a real measurement.

What it measures

Locomotion
Single-worm tracking across crawling, swimming and burrowing; population tracking with bout classification; swimming fatigue, healthspan decline, burrowing against graded resistance.
Sensory-guided behaviour
Mechanosensation and tap habituation, thermotaxis, chemotaxis and avoidance, magnetotaxis, area-restricted search, roaming versus dwelling.
Rhythmic programs
Pharyngeal pumping and the defecation motor program.
Calcium & cellular activity
Single- and multi-channel GCaMP extraction, AFD neuron tracking, cell position and orientation over time.
Anatomy & morphology
Body-wall muscle sarcomere morphometry with reviewable fibre tracing; non-striated muscle of the pharynx, uterus and anal depressor.
Acquisition & utilities
Scale and magnification calculators, sample-size planning, plate-orientation statistics, and a failure library for reviewing what went wrong on a run.

Getting started

  1. Run Setup_Lab_Tools.bat once on each lab computer.
  2. Launch Launch_Lab_Hub.bat — the front door to every tool.
  3. Read a tool's own description in the hub before its first run. That is the fastest way to know whether it fits your assay.

No programming is needed for day-to-day use. WINK checks for updates itself, so a lab machine stays current without a reinstall.

Under active development. Recent work includes a full striated-muscle morphometry module validated against the lab's own historical measurements, an in-app documentation assistant, and confocal z-stack support for 3D neurite tracing and muscle volume. Check the hub's changelog for what is current.
